# Project Scoring Model

## Overview

The Project Scoring Model is a comprehensive decision-support framework designed to objectively evaluate and prioritize projects based on multiple business, operational, financial, and strategic dimensions.

Organizations often manage numerous projects simultaneously, making it difficult to determine which initiatives should receive priority, resources, and investment. This model addresses that challenge by transforming diverse project characteristics into a single standardized score ranging from **0–100**, enabling transparent and data-driven project prioritization.

Unlike simple weighted scorecards, this framework incorporates statistical normalization, confidence adjustment, risk evaluation, and strategic weighting to produce an interpretable and scalable project quality score.

The model is modular, explainable, and fully customizable, making it suitable for organizations across industries.

# Model Architecture

The model evaluates every project across multiple independent dimensions.

Each dimension represents a key aspect of project success and organizational value.

The workflow follows the architecture below:

Project Data
↓

Feature Engineering
↓

Dimension-wise Scores
↓

Normalization
↓

Weighted Aggregation
↓

Risk & Opportunity Modifiers
↓

Final Project Score (0–100)

---

# Core Evaluation Dimensions

The model assesses projects across several major business dimensions.

## Strategic Alignment

Measures how closely the project supports organizational goals and long-term strategy.

Examples include:

- Business objectives
- Organizational vision
- Competitive positioning
- Market expansion
- Innovation initiatives

Projects with stronger strategic relevance receive higher scores.

---

## Business Impact

Evaluates the expected value generated by the project.

Examples:

- Revenue growth
- Cost reduction
- Customer satisfaction
- Productivity improvement
- Process optimization
- Competitive advantage

Higher organizational impact results in higher scores.

---

## Financial Performance

Assesses the project's financial viability.

Typical metrics include:

- Expected ROI
- Net business value
- Cost efficiency
- Budget utilization
- Expected profitability

Projects with stronger financial outcomes receive higher ratings.

---

## Project Complexity

Measures implementation difficulty.

Factors may include:

- Technical complexity
- Team size
- Cross-functional dependencies
- Integration challenges
- Infrastructure requirements

Higher complexity may reduce the final score unless balanced by significant business value.

---

## Resource Availability

Evaluates whether sufficient organizational resources are available.

Examples:

- Team capacity
- Budget availability
- Technology readiness
- Infrastructure support
- Skill availability

Projects that can be executed efficiently receive better scores.

---

## Time & Delivery

Measures schedule feasibility and expected delivery performance.

Examples:

- Estimated duration
- Deadline feasibility
- Milestone completion
- Time-to-value
- Delivery confidence

Projects with achievable timelines are prioritized.

---

## Risk Assessment

Captures uncertainties associated with project execution.

Potential risks include:

- Budget overruns
- Schedule delays
- Technical uncertainty
- Operational dependency
- Regulatory challenges
- Vendor risks

Higher risks result in score penalties.

---

## Stakeholder Value

Measures the value delivered to key stakeholders.

Examples:

- Customer benefit
- Executive sponsorship
- User adoption
- Departmental impact
- Organizational acceptance

Projects creating broader stakeholder value receive higher scores.

---

- Delivery confidence score

---

# Statistical Components

Several statistical techniques improve the robustness and reliability of the scoring model.

## Data Normalization

Each project metric exists on different numerical scales.

Normalization ensures:

- Fair comparison
- Stable weighting
- Balanced score contribution
- Consistent interpretation

---

## Weighted Aggregation

Every evaluation dimension contributes according to its business importance.

Organizations can customize weights depending on strategic priorities.

For example:

- Strategy-focused organizations may increase Strategic Alignment weight.
- Financial organizations may emphasize ROI.
- Operational teams may prioritize Delivery Confidence.

---

## Confidence Adjustment

Historical project performance is incorporated using confidence-adjusted estimates.

This prevents small historical samples from producing overly optimistic project scores.

Benefits include:

- More reliable predictions
- Reduced statistical noise
- Improved decision confidence

---

## Risk Penalty

Projects carrying significant uncertainty receive proportional score reductions.

Risk penalties prevent projects with unrealistic expectations from dominating rankings.

---

## Score Calibration

The final weighted score is calibrated onto a standardized **0–100 scale**, allowing easy comparison across all projects.

# Final Project Score

The final output is a standardized score between **0–100**.

Suggested interpretation:

| Score | Interpretation |
|--------|----------------|
| 90–100 | Exceptional Project |
| 75–89 | High Priority Project |
| 60–74 | Strong Candidate |
| 40–59 | Moderate Priority |
| 20–39 | Low Priority |
| 0–19 | Poor Investment Opportunity |

Organizations may customize these thresholds according to their portfolio strategy.
